Insight — Marketing

The importance of
marketing for business.

Marketing Strategy Growth

Marketing Insight

Read

Year

2025

Facts are forgotten. Stories are retold.

Your audience can't retain a feature list — but they never forget the shopkeeper who started with one cart, the trek that went wrong and was rescued, the grandmother's recipe behind a restaurant's best seller. Storytelling isn't a garnish on marketing; it's the format human memory actually uses. Two businesses can offer the same product. The one with a story is the one people repeat at dinner tables — and dinner tables are the oldest marketing channel there is.

The three beats every brand story needs.

A character. Not your company — your customer. The moment a story is about them, they lean in. A problem. Real, specific, felt this week — not "in today's fast-paced world." The sharper the problem, the more valuable the solution sounds. A transformation. Show the after: the queue outside the shop, the calm morning instead of the chaotic one, the confidence of a system that just works. People don't buy products; they buy the version of themselves on the other side.

You are the guide, not the hero.

The most common storytelling mistake brands make is casting themselves as the protagonist — awards, years of experience, passion. Customers skim past that. Position your brand as the guide: the one who's helped hundreds of people just like them through this exact problem. That single reframe changes a website from a brochure into an invitation, and it's the difference between copy that says "we're great" and copy that says "we know your road."

Start with three stories.

Every business already owns them: the origin story (why you started), the customer story (someone's before and after), and the product story (the problem a specific offer was built to kill). Write them once, tell them everywhere — site, socials, sales calls — and you'll never stare at a blank content calendar again.
