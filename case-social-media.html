Insight — Social

The impact of social media
on brand awareness.

Marketing Strategy Growth
Category

Marketing Insight

Read

4 minutes

Year

2025

What actually moves the needle.

Nobody buys from a stranger. Social media's real job is moving people along a short ladder: first they see you, then they recognise you, then they trust you enough to search your name. Awareness isn't the sale itself — it's the reason your name isn't foreign when the buying moment finally arrives. Every familiar impression is an inch of head start over the competitor they've never heard of.

Consistency beats virality. One viral post is a firework; three useful posts a week for a year is a lighthouse. Algorithms reward accounts that show up, and audiences quietly build trust on the accounts that never vanish for a month. Pick a rhythm you can survive a bad month at, and protect it.

Fewer platforms, done properly. A business spread thin across five platforms does all of them badly. One or two channels where your audience genuinely lives — with content shaped for how each platform behaves — outperforms copy-paste broadcasting every time.

The numbers that actually matter.

Followers are the scoreboard, not the game. Awareness shows up in: people searching your brand name, saves and shares (content useful enough to keep or send), profile visits after a post, and the oldest metric of all — "I saw you on Instagram" turning up in your DMs and on phone calls. If those rise, awareness is rising. If only follower count rises, you're collecting an audience, not building one.

The takeaway.

Social media doesn't close sales — it makes sales closeable. Treat it as the top of your funnel: show up consistently, look like the same brand everywhere, measure the signals of recognition. Do that for a year and the rest of your marketing gets cheaper, because you're no longer introducing yourself — you're greeting people who already know you.
